Maria Silk & Maria F. Scaroni
builds (but doesn’t make), moves (but doesn’t arrive), speaks (but doesn’t claim)

From my personal experience of queer motherhood, I find it important to think and act against the normativity of its institution and infrastructure. Against the dichotomy of being or not being (a parent) that oppresses every one of us, as though this is a singular crossroads of a decision threatening to limit our life experiences and the choice of values whichever way we take. Also, this decision is more or less possible for us depending on our class, gender, sexuality, biology, profession etc., often it is not a decision, but rather a series of circumstances, adaptations and survival strategies, an ongoing process of paradoxical relationships / relationships with the paradoxes of living. At the same time, the normative institution and infrastructure of parenthood takes away, to both those inside and those outside of it, the many spaces of the power of action. For that reason, we invited four artists and culture workers hailing from different artistic positions and cultural and social contexts they act in into this format. None of them is a mother in a normative way, but they build, move and speak about the experience of motherhood infrastructure, i.e. care, in different ways. Thinking about the infrastructures and paradigms of care, our wish is to open a space of alliance where we gather, not based on our similarities, but instead expanding the affective, performative, healing, collective registers we imagine within and between our languages and our bodies.

Some of the ideas we will be passing to one another are:
- motherhood as a performance – playing with the performativity of a (drag) ‘mother’
- biology as social infrastructure – what are the social consequences of the woman’s body possibility of reproduction when it is biologically over; how much does menopause influence on the social image and position of women, artists, their bodies and their work
- language and speech as the embodied infrastructure of imagination
What is it that builds (but doesn’t make), moves (but doesn’t arrive), speaks (but doesn’t claim)?
Or perhaps even better,
What is it like that which builds (but doesn’t make), moves (but doesn’t arrive), speaks (but doesn’t claim)?

Sonja Pregrad

 builds (but doesn’t make), moves (but doesn’t arrive), speaks (but doesn’t claim) through the performative apparatus it divulges the issues of the infrastructure of care towards finding the ways different bodies are imprinted or pushed out the idea of the infrastructure of motherhood. Which collective discussions on tenderness and care, belonging and separation, multiplication and duration, replaceability, continuation and rejection can be put to practice?

In part 1, together with the Sphynx and its question, the space of Studio Načeta closes the performance artist Maria Scaroni and drag queen and researcher Maria Silk in for a week. The doors open two times, to small performance liturgies, oracles, manifestations of life and pseudo-rituals to everyone present. The third time, the thus invoked frequency flows into the TALA PLE(j)S hall, turning into a two-headed Maria, a building, moving and talking entity.

In association with Sonja Pregrad

Maria Silk is an artist based in San Francisco. Centered on performance, her practice examines themes of collectivity and friction within queer and trans life, as well as received narratives around queer history and identity. Her work draws on over a decade of activity as a drag queen and DJ in San Francisco's queer nightlife. Silk has shown her work at the Berkeley Art Museum, the Cantor Center for the Arts, CounterPulse, Slash, and Southern Exposure, and internationally at Fierce! Festival 2019 (Birmingham, UK), and Improspekcije 2024. Silk also writes on performance, queer and trans history, sexual cultures, and the countercultural and artistic legacies of San Francisco and the Bay Area, with publications in BUTT, GLQ, and Open Space. She is known as a very good sport.


Maria F. Scaroni (IT/DE) is a dance artist, living in Berlin since 2004. She creates and interprets choreographic works rooted in improvisational practices and altered states of consciousness. Maria hosts workshops re-purposing post-modern dance legacies towards technologies for mutual empowerment, crossbreeding somatic practices and anti-oppression frameworks, storytelling and theory, to bring somatic competence in support of a culture of connection. Maria performs in Meg Stuart’s works (Poltics of Ecstasy, Auf den Tisch!, Built to Last, Sketches/Notebook, Until Our Hearts Stop, City Lights, Tanzkongress, Solos&Duos, Waterworks) with whom she collaborates since 2009. After collective Lecken, an erogenous queer rave, since pandemic she has been hosting Social Pleasure Center, a community space for somatic post-activism, queer feminist joyful militancy, radical redistribution of resources and temporary social choreography. She currently creates (Unsex Me Here) and teaches (Somatic Drag) with icon drag queen Olympia Bukkakis.
